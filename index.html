A Goodbye We Did Not Want to Write

September 2026

To our teammates and their families: you have been one of the best parts of our fall, and this page is not about any of you. The players, the parents in the stands, the volunteers who keep a season running, all of that has been a joy. This is about a decision our family had to make for one kid, and we want to be straight with you about why.

Over the first weekend of games, we found ourselves writing down every shift, and what we saw is laid out below. In all four games, #24 got far less ice time than any other skater. Reviewing the footage to log the times was heartbreaking in its own way: long stretches of #24 fidgeting to pass the time, or lying on the bench. We don't claim to know the reasons behind it, and we didn't want to spend a season finding out at his expense. What we lost is the trust a season is built on.

We came into this season all in, which is why we brought the best training tools, like Power Edge Pro, to team practices. Walking away from something we put that much heart into is hard, and if this news frustrates or disappoints you, we understand; we sat with those same feelings before making this decision. We hope the rest of your season is a great one, and we'll see you around.

#24 finished last in ice time in every game

Over the weekend of September 18 to 20, the team played four games with eleven skaters dressed, and we logged every shift by its start and end time on the game clock. In each of those four games, #24 finished with the least ice time on the team. No other skater finished last more than once.

Most of the gap traces to long stretches on the bench: in ten of the twelve periods, #24 sat through a continuous stretch of 3:12 or longer. Who was standing behind the bench mattered just as much. The head coach ran the bench in games 1, 3, and 4, and in those games #24 averaged 9:59 of ice time, between 50% and 70% of an even forward share. In game 2 the head coach was not on the bench, and #24 played 13:39, which was his most ice time of the weekend, 3:40 above his average in the other three games, and 82% of an even share. The one game without the head coach was the one game #24 came close to a normal rotation.

The games, one by one

The first chart combines all four games. The game charts that follow group forwards and defense separately, since the two positions rotate on different clocks: three forward spots split among seven forwards, and two defense spots split among four defensemen. The dashed line in each group marks an even share for that group.

About the numbers: shifts were logged from game footage by game-clock start and end times. Games 1, 2, and 4 were 39:00 of stop-time hockey, played in 12, 12, and 15 minute periods. In game 3 the clock ran through stoppages from 12:41 of the third period on, so its figures count play time only, which came to 35:58. Two skaters, 17 and 29, played forward in some games and defense in others; each chart groups them by the position they played that game.
